## 1. Scope & Purpose
This operational procedure defines standard operating thresholds, thermal diagnostics, and status classification for continuous and intermittent gas lift wells operated across all company asset areas (Area A, Area B, Area C).

## 2. Thermal Production Verification Rule
Gas lift operations introduce compressed gas into the annulus to lift reservoir hydrocarbons. Because reservoir fluids rise from subterranean formations (typically 130°F–180°F at reservoir depths), active fluid production creates a significant thermal signature at the surface wellhead compared to ambient air temperature.

### 2.1 The Temperature Differential Rule
- **Wellhead Temperature ($T_{wellhead}$):** Surface temperature reading in degrees Fahrenheit (°F) measured at the wing valve.
- **Ambient Air Temperature ($T_{ambient}$):** Local air temperature at the time of inspection in degrees Fahrenheit (°F).
- **Thermal Differential ($\Delta T$):** $\Delta T = T_{wellhead} - T_{ambient}$.

### 2.2 Threshold for Gas Lift Wells
- **Threshold Limit:** **15.0°F**.
- **Classification Criteria:**
  - If $\Delta T \ge 15.0^\circ\text{F}$: The wellhead exhibits thermal flow signature. The well is classified as **PRODUCING**.
  - If $\Delta T < 15.0^\circ\text{F}$: Fluid column is stagnant; gas injection is failing to circulate warm reservoir fluid. The well is classified as **NOT PRODUCING**.
  - **Exception - Planned Maintenance:** If the well is shut down under an active, pre-approved Work Permit or Scheduled Maintenance window, the well is classified as **PLANNED SHUT-IN** rather than unplanned downtime.

## 3. Weekly Inspection and Downtime Accounting Rules
1. Weekly inspections take place on designated inspection days.
2. A single weekly inspection confirming that a well is **Not Producing** accounts for **7 days (168 hours) of downtime**.
3. Two consecutive weekly inspections confirming the well is **Not Producing** account for **14 days (336 hours) of downtime**.
4. A **missed inspection** does not imply production or shutdown; under official accounting rules, a missed inspection contributes **0 days of additional downtime**.
5. Repair time recorded on work tickets is tracked for maintenance cost metrics, but is **NOT** the regulatory downtime accounting method.
